# KIN — Concept

## The insight

Modern social products make it easy to discover strangers. Ancestry products make it possible to discover family connections. KIN explores the space between them.

**You just walked past your cousin.**

The product hypothesis is simple: discover a possible family connection nearby before revealing identity.

## The staged reveal

1. **Signal** — a possible connection exists nearby, without name, photo or exact location.
2. **Context** — possible relationship, approximate distance and connection confidence.
3. **Choice** — explore without exposing identity.
4. **Mutual reveal** — identity appears only after both people choose to connect.
5. **Connect** — continue with a lightweight conversation.

## Positioning

KIN is not a replacement for genealogy services, DNA testing or social networks. It explores a missing interaction layer between them:

**Ancestry tells you where you come from. Social networks tell you who you know. KIN asks who you might be connected to right now.**

## Novelty question

The idea should be evaluated honestly against genealogy, DNA matching, location-based social discovery and family-finder products. The potential distinction is the interaction model: **nearby discovery → anonymous possible kinship → mutual consent → identity reveal → social connection**.

## Product language

Use: possible, may, connection confidence, approximate distance, mutual reveal.

Avoid claims of confirmed biological kinship.
